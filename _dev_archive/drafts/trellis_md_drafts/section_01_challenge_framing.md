# Section 1. Challenge framing

*Approximately 200 words. Drafted from the project's master challenge statement; track-changes record below.*

---

Trellis is an open-source, child-centric platform for **modelling the linkages between industrial pollution, environmental damage, and the health of populations exposed to them.** It fuses satellite Earth Observation, ground-based DePIN sensors, and routine health-system data to build trend models across two coupled domains: **(1) pollutant and environmental exposure** — atmospheric (NO₂, particulate matter, gas-flare radiative heat), terrestrial (oil-spill footprints, land-use change), and hydrological (flooding, water-table contamination); and **(2) child-health outcomes** — vector-borne disease (childhood malaria), acute respiratory infection and pneumonia, and the chronic-onset development of asthma and other lung diseases in children under five.

The platform's specific contribution is a **4-week ahead, ward-resolution forecast** that links exposure and outcome in one analytical surface, designed to be used by state ministries of health, schools, primary care providers, and caregivers. It builds on the place-based-determinants-of-health tradition (Snow 1854; Belsky et al. 2019, *Nature Human Behaviour*) and the operational lineage of WHO EWARS, the EPIDEMIA Ethiopia malaria-forecasting system (Wimberly et al.), and the Allegheny County Pediatric Asthma Study (UPMC). The novelty is sub-district resolution, an open-source DePIN sensor fusion layer that fills satellite gaps, and the explicit toddler-asthma cohort focus that no operational climate-health platform in West Africa currently serves.

## Provenance and track-changes record

This section is derived from the project's master challenge statement, provided by Patrick on 9 May 2026:

> Platforms to model trends and develop existing linkages between pollution and environmental damage with epidemiological modeling on disease spread and impact on health and food supply, as well as correlations with asthma development in toddlers and other lung diseases.

Edits applied during refinement, in order of impact on meaning:

- **"develop existing linkages"** → **"modelling the linkages"**. Reason: in scientific framing, *develop* is ambiguous (find / strengthen / create); *modelling* is what the platform actually does and matches the EOI methodology language.
- **"correlations with asthma development"** → **"chronic-onset development of asthma"**. Reason: *correlations* is statistical language that under-claims; the medical literature on early-childhood respiratory exposure uses *chronic-onset development* as the standard term.
- **"toddlers"** → operationalised as **"children under five"**. Reason: the standard public-health cohort definition and the cohort that WorldPop, UNICEF, and DHIS2 all measure.
- **"impact on food supply"** → **removed**. Reason: per Patrick's instruction (9 May 2026), the food-system scope is dropped from the current sprint and will be considered for a later phase.
- **"impact on health"** → **"child-health outcomes"**. Reason: Trellis is child-centric per trellis.md; *health* is too broad and *child-health outcomes* matches the project's stated focus.
- **"epidemiological modeling on disease spread"** → **the two specific disease classes** (vector-borne, acute respiratory, chronic-onset). Reason: epidemiological modelling is a method, not a scope; the specific disease classes name what the model predicts.
- **Added** the methodological lineage paragraph (Snow / Belsky / EWARS / EPIDEMIA / Allegheny County) per project instructions Section 5 to anchor the academic citation.
- **Added** the explicit "novelty" sentence at the end to set up Section 12 (novelty levers).

If any of these substitutions do not match Patrick's intent, the original statement is preserved verbatim above and any edit can be reverted on request.
